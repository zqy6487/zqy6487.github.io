About me

I'm Qiyun (Claire) Zheng, a student at Shanghai Foreign Language School Affiliated to SISU. My work brings together robotics, environmental engineering, and collaborative problem-solving.

I design robots to explore complex environments, from competition fields to wetlands. I developed an ellipsoidal robot for wetland ecological monitoring, which received a First Award in Robotics and Intelligent Machines at Regeneron ISEF 2026.

My paper on this work has been accepted to the 2026 IEEE ICDMW Teen Research Track.

With FRC Team 6487, I help turn ideas into working machines and build a community where teammates can learn from one another. Beyond engineering, I play the guqin, perform on stage, and take part in conversations about sustainability.

Interests: Robotics & autonomous navigation; environmental monitoring; AI for ecological data.

Research

An Ellipsoidal Robot for Wetland Ecological Monitoring

Jan 2025 — Present

Inspired by field inspections at Chongming Dongtan Wetland and Xinjiangwan Wetland Ecological Park, I designed and iteratively prototyped a remote-controlled ellipsoidal robot for complex wetland terrain.

  • Tested the system in laboratory and field environments, collecting environmental data to evaluate its performance.
  • Designed an AI agent to explain wetland conditions and analyze data gathered by the robot.
  • Filed a design patent for the robot's structural configuration; the application is under review.

First Award, Regeneron ISEF, 2026.

Follow-up fieldwork in Beihai, Guangxi

Aug 2026

Following this project, I spent three days visiting coastal wetlands and sites along the G228 highway in Beihai. I observed waterbirds on tidal flats and around aquaculture ponds, learned benthic quadrat sampling under guidance, and spoke with local residents about the road and their daily travel. Seeing the tide cover our sampling area made the changing conditions of wetland fieldwork especially tangible.

Conversations with local conservation practitioners helped me reconsider who would use the robot and where it could operate. Their feedback highlighted bird disturbance, muddy terrain, tides, cost, and maintenance as questions for further design work. I also visited the Guangxi Mangrove Research Center to learn about aquaculture water treatment and constructed mangrove wetlands. These visits are informing my next steps toward practical, low-disturbance wetland monitoring.
